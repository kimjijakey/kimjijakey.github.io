Jacob Kim

Music Industry

Hello! My name is Jacob Kim, and I hope to concentrate in the music industry for my major. I have an extensive history in music production, play the piano, and have been self-teaching myself how to use Ableton Live over the past 5 years. My compositional style is mostly instrumental, because I enjoy composing soundtracks that could potentially be used in video games or movies. I also aim to become a self-taught video game developer, so I am also teaching myself how to code on-the-side.

My journey in music started when I was four years old, when I first took piano lessons with a private tutor/family friend from church. Although I didn’t know it at the time, I was very lucky to have such a resource available to me at that time. From 2012 to 2020, I worked on becoming a better piano player, participating in piano recitals and practicing often. During COVID, I had unfortunately lost interest in playing the piano, so I stopped for almost two years. After that time, I decided that I’d get back into piano by learning songs that interested me, and ever since then I have been off-and-on with practicing piano.

Eventually, I learned about Ableton Live and music production from a recorded conference with Daniel Rosenfeld, who is the composer for the soundtrack of the popular video game Minecraft, and Toby Fox, the solo developer of UNDERTALE. I thought music production looked fun, so I took it up and downloaded Ableton Live myself.

Although I wouldn’t exactly count my work as “professional experience” yet, I have been commissioned by small video game development studios and friends to compose music for their own projects. I also attended UCI x GATI’s GSET program, which was a summer program that taught students the principles behind game design and collaboration. I composed the soundtrack to my group’s game while I attended GSET. In my free time, I compose music for my own personal video game development projects too.
